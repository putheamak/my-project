What's the best way to store leftovers so they don't dry out?

The container does most of the work. If air gets in, texture goes out.

  • Glass storage containers with locking lids — stack neatly in the fridge, go straight from fridge to microwave, and don't hold onto curry smells the way old plastic does. The PrepNaturals glass containers come in a 5-pack.
  • Silicone stretch lids — pull over a bowl, a cut melon, or a pan you can't be bothered to decant. They turn any dish you already own into a storage container. The Longzon 14-pack stretch lids cover small bowls up to big ones; Goodful silicone suction lids sit flat on a pot so the whole thing can go in the fridge.
  • Reusable silicone food bags — good for soft things like cooked rice, shredded chicken or sauce you want to lay flat.
  • Airtight containers with a sealing clip or clamp — for anything you want to keep crisp-ish, like cooked bacon or roasted nuts.

One habit that beats any gadget: pack leftovers into the container as soon as the meal is plated, not after you've watched an episode of something. Cooling and sealing promptly keeps food in better shape and saves you a second clean-up trip later.

How do you freeze leftovers in single portions?

This is the step most people skip, and it's why frozen leftovers turn into a mystery brick nobody wants to defrost.

  • Silicone freezer portion trays — freeze soup, stew, chilli or pasta sauce in one-cup blocks, then pop them out into a bag. Defrost exactly one serving instead of a family-size slab. A silicone freezer tray with lid does this well.
  • Ice cube trays with lids — perfect for leftover stock, coconut milk, pesto, herbs in oil, or that half-can of tomato paste. Try the Airabc silicone ice cube trays with lids.
  • Reusable labels or a dry-erase marker set — write what it is and the date. Future you will not remember. A Phomemo D30 mini label maker prints labels from your phone.
  • Flat-freezing bags plus a cooling rack — freeze bags flat so they stack like files instead of lumps.

What gadgets make reheating leftovers taste better?

Reheating is where most leftovers die. A few cheap things help a lot:

  • Vented microwave cover — traps steam so rice and pasta rehydrate instead of hardening, and your microwave stays clean. The HOVER COVER magnetic splatter cover lives on the microwave ceiling; the Aidacom 2-in-1 cover doubles as a silicone mat and trivet.
  • Microwave steamer basket — brings tired vegetables and rice back to life with a splash of water. A collapsible silicone microwave steamer folds flat; the GoodCook microwave steamer has an adjustable steam vent.
  • Small nonstick or cast-iron skillet — the fix for anything that was ever crispy. Pizza, roast potatoes, breaded chicken.
  • Oil mister / spray bottle — a light spray before reheating in a pan or air fryer helps surfaces crisp instead of drying out.

Frequently asked questions

Are glass or plastic containers better for leftovers?

Glass is heavier but doesn't stain, warp or hold odours, and it handles reheating well. Plastic is lighter for carrying lunches to work. Many people end up with both: glass at home, light containers in the bag, such as the Rubbermaid Brilliance containers. Check each product's listing on Amazon for microwave, freezer and dishwasher guidance before you buy.

How long can you keep leftovers in the fridge?

Follow official food safety guidance from your local food safety authority rather than a blog — the recommended window depends on the food. Labelling the date on the container is the simplest way to stay on top of it.

Do I really need freezer portion trays?

Not strictly — a muffin tin and a bag does the same job. But silicone trays release frozen blocks far more easily and usually come with a lid, so you don't get freezer burn or spills. If you cook big pots of anything, they earn their space quickly.

Will a microwave cover actually change the taste?

It changes the texture, which is most of the problem. Trapped steam stops the surface of rice, pasta and roasted vegetables from drying into a crust. It also means you're not wiping down the inside of the microwave twice a week. A Le'raze glass splatter cover sits right over the bowl and holds the steam in.
