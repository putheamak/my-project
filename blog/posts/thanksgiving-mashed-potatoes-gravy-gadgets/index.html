How do I get mashed potatoes smooth instead of gluey?

Use a ricer, not a mixer, which can overwork potatoes into paste. A ricer presses them through small holes so they come out fine and even; fold in warm butter and milk after.

  • PriorityChef 15 oz stainless steel potato ricer: a press-style ricer that also handles sweet potatoes and cooked cauliflower. Skip it if you want texture in your mash.
  • 2-piece stainless steel potato masher set: the simpler tool for chunky mash, used all year for avocado and ground beef. Skip it if lumps are a dealbreaker at your table.
  • AUOON clip-on silicone strainer for pots: clips onto the pot rim so you can tip out the water without hauling out a colander or needing a second pair of hands. Skip it if your pots have unusually thick or flared rims.

Steam-dry tip: drain the potatoes, then let them sit in the hot, empty pot for a minute or two so the surface water steams off. Wet potatoes make loose, watery mash whichever tool you use.

How do I keep gravy from turning greasy or lumpy?

Grease and lumps are separate problems with separate fixes.

For grease, pour the pan drippings into a separator and wait a couple of minutes while the fat floats up. A bottom-release model drains the flavorful juices from underneath, so you stop before the fat reaches the valve.

  • 4-cup fat and gravy separator with bottom release: a 4-cup size for holiday drippings, with a bottom release so the fat stays behind. Skip it if you make gravy from a jar or a packet.

For lumps, either whisk as you go or blend the finished gravy in the saucepan.

  • 3-pack stainless steel whisk set: keep the flour moving as the liquid goes in, the classic no-outlet way to stop lumps.
  • Turelar 2-speed corded immersion blender (400W): the rescue tool once lumps happen. Blend right in the pot, then use it for cranberry sauce or soup. Skip it if you have no free outlet near the stove.

Frequently asked questions

Ricer or masher: do I need both?

No. Get the ricer for smooth mash or the masher for texture. If you want one tool you'll use all year, the masher is more flexible.

Can I just skim the fat with a spoon instead of using a separator?

You can. A separator is faster on a crowded stove, since the fat rises on its own while you handle the potatoes.

Will a stick blender splash hot gravy everywhere?

Keep the blade fully submerged before switching it on and start on low speed. Check the shaft length in the listing against your saucepan.
