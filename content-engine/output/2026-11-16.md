### Try this tonight: cook the whole dinner in one 8-inch pan

1. Heat the pan on medium and cook your protein first — an egg, a sausage, a handful of shrimp. Move it to your plate.
2. In the same pan, cook chopped vegetables in the leftover fat for a few minutes, then add a splash of water and cover for two minutes so they steam.
3. Put the protein back in, add a pinch of salt and whatever sauce is in the fridge door, and eat it straight from the plate you already dirtied.

### What to look for in first apartment kitchen gadgets

- **Size that matches one person.** An 8-inch pan and a 1.5-quart pot cook a single portion fast and fit a small sink. Large cookware sits unused in a tiny kitchen.
- **Storage shape.** Collapsible, stackable or flat-hanging tools beat rigid sets when there's one drawer and one cabinet.
- **Cleaning without a dishwasher.** Most first apartments don't have one. Check the listing for dishwasher-safe or simple hand-wash construction with no awkward crevices.
- **One tool, several jobs.** A zester that also does garlic, or ramekins that bake and store, reduce how much gear has to be bought at all.

### What I left out, and why

- **Stand mixers and food processors:** too big for a first apartment counter and usually well over this list's budget.
- **Full cookware sets:** they duplicate the two pans a one-person kitchen actually uses.
- **Knife blocks:** they eat counter space; blade guards in a drawer do the same job.

If they eat rice more than once a week, yes. A mini cooker handles one or two portions without babysitting a pot, which matters when you're also doing everything else.
