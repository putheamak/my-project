### Try this tonight: a 10-minute emergency check

1. Walk through the house and collect every flashlight in one place. Switch each one on, and replace or charge the batteries of any that are dim.
2. Fill a few clean bottles or jugs with tap water, write today's date on them and put them somewhere cool and dark. Aim for about 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day.
3. Write the most important phone numbers (family, doctor, neighbors) on paper and put it with the flashlights, because a dead phone takes your contacts with it.

That's the start of a kit already. The gadgets below fill the gaps.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How long should my emergency kit last?

Start with 3 days, which matches the EU, UK and US advice and covers most short emergencies. Then build toward a week, as Sweden, Norway and Japan now suggest. Germany advises up to 10 days.

#### How much water should I store?

About 4 liters (1 gallon) per person per day for drinking and basic hygiene. For a family of three for 3 days, that's about 36 liters. Store extra for pets.

#### What else should be in the kit besides gadgets?

Food that needs no fridge (canned fish, beans, peanut butter, crackers, dried fruit), a week of medicines, cash in small notes, warm clothes, hygiene supplies, garbage bags, and a paper list of phone numbers. Add one or two items to each weekly shop rather than buying everything at once, and don't panic-buy.

Check your own government's latest advice: [Ready.gov](https://www.ready.gov/kit) in the US, [gov.uk/prepare](https://www.gov.uk/prepare) in the UK, and your national civil protection agency elsewhere.
