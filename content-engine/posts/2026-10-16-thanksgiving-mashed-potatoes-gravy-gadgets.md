### What's the fastest way to get lump-free mashed potatoes?

### How do you keep Thanksgiving gravy from being greasy or lumpy?

Two separate problems, two cheap fixes.

Grease: pour the pan drippings into a separator and let them sit a couple of minutes. The fat floats; a bottom-release model lets you pour off the good stuff from underneath and leave the fat behind.

Lumps: instead of straining and whisking and swearing, blend the gravy right in the saucepan with a stick blender. It's designed to smooth it in the pot in well under a minute.

### Frequently asked questions

#### Ricer or masher: which one should I get?
A ricer is designed for smooth, fluffy mash with no lumps; a masher leaves some texture, which a lot of families prefer. If you only want one tool for the whole year, the masher is more flexible; if smooth mash is the goal, get the ricer.

#### Can I use a ricer for anything besides potatoes?
Yes — it works for cooked cauliflower, sweet potatoes and squash, and some people use it to press excess water out of cooked greens. It's a Thanksgiving buy that doesn't sit in a drawer for eleven months.

#### Do I really need a gravy separator, or can I just skim with a spoon?
You can skim with a spoon, and plenty of people do. A separator is designed to be faster when you're working against the clock, since you pour the drippings in and let the fat rise on its own.

#### Will an immersion blender fit in my saucepan?
Check the listing for the shaft length and compare it to the depth of your pot. Keep the blade fully submerged while it's running so hot gravy doesn't splash, and start on the lower speed.

#### What should I buy if I'm hosting Friendsgiving in a small apartment?
Prioritize tools that don't need oven space: the slow cooker, the ramekins and the clip-on strainer.
