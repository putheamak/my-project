### What makes a good kitchen stocking stuffer?

Three things, in order:

- **Small.** It has to physically fit in a stocking or a gift bag.
- **Used weekly, not yearly.** Spatulas, strainers, scoops - not a single-purpose novelty.
- **No instruction manual required.** Nobody wants homework on Christmas morning.

### Frequently asked questions

#### How much should I spend on a stocking stuffer?
Most people buy a few small things per stocking rather than one big item. Since Amazon prices change constantly, check the current price on Amazon and set your own ceiling before you add things to the cart.

#### Are kitchen gadgets a boring gift?
Only if they're flimsy. A good spatula set or a strainer that gets used three times a week beats a scented candle by February. Pair a practical tool with something fun - a popcorn popper plus seasonings, for example - and it reads as a gift, not a chore.

#### What if they already own one?
Doubles of consumable-feeling items are fine: spatulas, strainers, reusable bags and tea infusers all wear out or get lost. Avoid duplicating anything with a motor or a cord unless you know for sure.

#### Can I use these for Secret Santa or White Elephant instead?
Yes. The popcorn popper and seasoning combo, the frother and the ice cream scoop all work as standalone swap gifts because they're instantly recognizable and nobody has to pretend to like them.
