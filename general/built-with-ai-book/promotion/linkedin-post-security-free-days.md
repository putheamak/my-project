🔐 Insecurity in the age of AI

AI already lets one person do what once needed a team. But that same convenience creates a new kind of insecurity. Every new AI tool asks for access: to our email, our files, our customers and our accounts. And because convenience is our default, most of us say yes without reading the conditions. AI rewards us for sharing more. Cybersecurity protects us by sharing less.

At my former job, my first real success with AI was a small program that analysed customer satisfaction surveys. One detail mattered more than the code: I never gave the AI our real data. That data belonged to the company and to the customers who had trusted us with their answers. So I created a test dataset with invented responses in exactly the same format. The AI wrote the program using only that test data. I then ran it on the real data offline, on my own computer, where it never left the company.

Today the question is not whether AI tools can do the work. A great many can. The real question is one each of us must answer for ourselves: how much of our real data are we willing to expose for the sake of convenience?
