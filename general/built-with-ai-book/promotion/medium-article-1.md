# Medium article 1

**Title:** I'm Not a Programmer. Here Is What I Built With AI for $31 a Month — and What It Didn't Do

**Subtitle:** Four real tools, one small business in Phnom Penh, and the numbers nobody puts in the advertisements

**Tags (Medium allows 5):** Artificial Intelligence, Small Business, No Code, Entrepreneurship, Productivity

---

In August 2026, I left my job as a research supervisor. A few weeks later, I opened a small parcel delivery business in Phnom Penh, Cambodia.

I studied computer science at university, but my major was networking. I have never worked as a programmer. Yet over the next two months I built four working tools with an AI assistant. I want to share what they were, what they really cost, and — the part most AI posts leave out — what happened next.

## The four tools

**1. A booking app inside Telegram.** Most of our customers are online sellers. They book deliveries by sending voice messages, and someone in our office has to listen, ask questions and type everything again. So I described the problem to the AI in plain words, and together we built a small form that opens inside Telegram. The seller fills it in, in Khmer, and the booking arrives in our office group with a booking code. It runs on a free plan and costs nothing per month.

**2. A blog that drafts its own posts.** I wanted to test the famous promise of "passive income" with Amazon affiliate links. The AI built an engine that drafts a post three times a week about kitchen gadgets under $30, and checks each draft against Amazon's rules. Nothing goes live until I read it and say yes.

**3. A video-ad factory.** I could not afford a video editor. So each ad is designed like a simple web page, and a script turns it into a short vertical video — Khmer first, short English below — with the same correct phone numbers and prices every time.

**4. The system that helped me write a book.** Notes from me, structure and drafts from the AI, and every chapter read and corrected by me. The result is now on Amazon.

## What it cost

The tools cost about **$31 a month**: $20 for the AI subscription, about $10 for the AI service my blog engine uses automatically (an estimate — my first real bill will tell), and a domain name at $12 a year. Hosting, the booking app and the code storage all run on free plans.

But that number is misleading on its own. The real cost was **time**: almost two months, usually 4 to 8 hours a day, describing problems, testing, fixing mistakes and learning how each system works. Anyone who tells you AI tools are "free" is not counting their own hours.

## What happened next (the honest numbers, as of 31 October)

- **Booking app:** 32 bookings in our first month. A small start — but every one arrived complete, with nothing to listen to and nothing to type again. The hard part is not the technology. It is changing the habits of sellers who have sent voice messages for years.
- **Blog:** 27 posts. 58 real visits in twelve days. **Zero sales.** Amazon gives new affiliates 180 days to make three sales, so the clock is running.
- **Video ads:** our first paid test brought 2,546 views, 57 clicks and **zero bookings**. Since then I have posted 17 short videos on our Facebook page, and I learned more from that zero than from any course.
- **The book:** published on 12 November 2026.

## Five things I learned

1. **Describe the problem, not the solution.** When I explained *what my customers struggle with*, the AI often suggested something better than what I had in mind.
2. **Write the rules down before the work begins.** In its first drafts, the AI happily promised things my business had never decided to offer. Now a short rules file says: never invent prices, offers or promises.
3. **Check everything yourself.** The AI could not see that symbols in my videos had turned into empty boxes. I could, because I watched every video.
4. **Never give it data that is not yours to give.** When I automated my old survey analysis, I built the tool with invented test data and ran it on the real data offline.
5. **Small steps, and approve before it acts.** An AI agent can change dozens of files in seconds. That is exciting — until something breaks and you cannot tell which change did it.

## Why I am sharing this from Cambodia

Most stories about AI come from Silicon Valley or London. But most of the world's workers live in countries like mine, where fewer people use AI, fewer courses are in our language, and many jobs are exactly the kind of work AI will change first.

I am not an expert. I am an ordinary small-business owner who decided to learn by doing. If I can build these tools, many more people can — if they start.
